Demand-aware producers and consumers
====================================

Observation contracts
---------------------

The dialect adapter owns admission. An inactive or unimported operation remains
uninterpreted; it does not acquire streaming behavior merely because another
profile has that operation. In particular, plain HE leaves `once` alone. HE and
PeTTa retain their respective value and raise boundaries in every active route.

The rows below describe the existing contracts that a shared implementation
must preserve. "Prefix" includes all work needed to reach the first accepted
answer, not merely the first candidate or firing. "Close" releases unused
continuations and retained views; it does not execute them or undo earlier
writes. Unless a space declares an order, a complete answer collection is a
bag, not a license to select a different first answer under an ordered policy.

| Producer / consumer | Operand and alternative observations | Answer, bindings and errors | Demand, effects and close |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `superpose` (HE/extended, PeTTa) | The operand is evaluated as the dialect requires before its choice frontier is available. Alternatives retain source order where an ordered observer requires it; a complete unordered bag need not acquire hash order as a new policy. | Each selected expression or completed value retains the adapter's environment and provenance. HE errors remain values subject to its completion policy; PeTTa raises remain control, not ordinary items. | First demand enters only the required prefix. Answerless branches can already have written. Closing skips optional later branches without rollback. |
| `collapse` | It consumes the producer's complete frontier in the selected policy order. It does not make the producer's operands lazy. | HE applies normal-value preference to its collected frontier; all-error collections retain error multiplicity. PeTTa copies each answer with its own variables and discards no duplicate. The collection has the collector's empty outer environment. | All demand exhausts the producer unless evaluation interrupts. `collapse(once(P))` contains at most the first answer of P, while preserving effects of P's answerless prefix. |
| `once` | It observes alternatives, not proposals: rejected candidates and answerless firings precede the answer. Required operand evaluation still occurs before the first alternative. | It returns one completed answer with that answer's retained bindings. Zero answers has the dialect's ordinary empty observation. HE error preference is not replaced by a global one-row limiter; PeTTa faults follow their handler. | Acceptance closes the residual producer and never runs its unused suffix. Earlier effects, including an invalid-destination failure after a take, remain in their established order. |
| `foldall` (PeTTa lowering) and the shared fold worker | The generator is consumed in its established order. Current native boundaries
-------------------------

`CettaOwnedExecution`, `CettaOwnedReturn`, `CettaOwnedBind` and
`CettaCallReturnCursor` already provide return, completion, pause, handoff,
interruption, acceptance and cancellation. Prepared pure consumers and the
PeTTa search-machine adapters use those types. The ordered outcome visitor is
the HE/public evaluator adapter; it supplies first versus ordered-stream versus
finite-prefix demand without introducing another evaluator or wrapping every
scalar in a heap object.

`stream_emit` carries bounded demand. Its transform-specific first route calls
`cetta_eval_transform_first`; the remaining direct and dialect paths retain
their existing admission. Public `tf:propose` is deliberately a materializing
collector and remains so.

Before the resumable-entry specialization, `cetta_eval_transform_first` called
`cetta_transform_propose` before its firing loop. `tf_walk` and
`tf_keep_candidate` therefore built the complete proposal product even when
the first candidate answered. That is the candidate-production defect addressed
here, not a request to make arbitrary HE operands lazy.

The admitted first-answer route now opens `CettaTransformCandidateCursor` and
requests one `(tf:cand GUIDE)` at a time. Each conjunct has an explicit level,
an unstarted occurrence view, an active position-preserving clone, binding and
arena marks, and its footprint length. Returning a proposal restores the last
level's candidate marks and decrements the depth; exhausting a level restores
its parent. Thus a rejected firing resumes the actual next match rather than
re-emitting a partial guide or restarting the product.

Every level's initial view is captured before the first firing, using its
source pattern before later bindings narrow it. That frontier is a superset of
the matches available under those later bindings; matching still performs the
actual unification. Re-entering a level clones its unstarted view. Later
inserts therefore do not join late, deleted rows can remain discoverable, and
phase-one live validation separately decides whether a captured firing remains
enabled. Public `tf:propose` remains materializing, and no cursor atom is
exposed to a program. For an eligible fragment whose store-cardinality
upper-bound product reaches 64, the all-demand collector exhausts the cursor
into the public bag. Smaller products retain the cheaper eager collector;
this choice is made before constructing cursor pins or traversal arrays, and
first-answer evaluation streams at every size. The established reads-only bag
fast path and all ineligible walkers remain in place.

`CETTA_TRANSFORM_CURSOR_REFERENCE=1` permanently selects the former
materializing proposal route for both first-answer evaluation and public
collection in the same binary. The differential gate compares complete stdout,
termination and diagnostics after normalizing fresh variable identities and
space addresses. Its counter gates additionally reject an implementation that
hides an eager fallback behind equal answers.

The counters separate open/close ownership, completed entry candidates,
occurrence rows yielded to matching, proposal-guide allocation work, and two
byte accounts. `transform-cursor-owned-bytes-peak` covers the cursor, levels,
plan/entry capacities, directly owned binding-builder vectors, any heap-grown
frame-identity vector, traversal arrays and scratch arena. Shared syntax owners
and cached/shared lookup, frame and Prime occurrence payloads are outside that
vector account. `transform-cursor-accounted-bytes-peak` adds each distinct
detached view's row and removal-mark storage once. It does not charge live store
rows, shared trie/head indexes or dense kept-fact storage to the cursor, so it
is an exact account of the named components, not a total RSS measurement. In
the taking acceptance case the owned peak is unchanged from n to 2n while the
entry-inclusive account grows with detached rows. High-water sampling occurs
before binding/arena rollback and before active occurrence-view release, so
failed matches and a traversal with no proposals cannot lose their transient
peak merely because later cleanup frees the storage.

`test-transform-stream-control` checks 40,000 and 160,000 answer products with
a successful first candidate: one candidate and two occurrence rows in each.
It also covers a rejected prefix through cursor exhaustion, an empty producer,
a 200 by 200 disjoint traversal yielding no proposals, an empty first
public-proposal frontier followed by a nonempty one, 100 repeated early closes,
nested answerless firings, later insert/delete/reinsert, equal copies, overlapping read/take footprints, irrelevant writes, function and
invalid destinations, captured bindings, dynamic-space and `let` barriers,
full-drain controls, and a seeded differential corpus. A same-objects fault
executable refuses the second outer row's inner cursor clone; the first
answerless effect must remain exactly once, with no whole-transform fallback.
The cursor counter gates run under ASan/UBSan as well as in the ordinary build.

Evaluation barriers and retained views
--------------------------------------

The eligible internal cursor admits only a static run of ordinary read/take
conjuncts. It captures occurrence views before the first firing and yields one
copied proposal at a time. Later firings cannot make an unopened join leg see
appended entry rows. Live validation still decides whether a captured take can
fire; the view is not a reservation or a substitute for phase-one publication.

A `let`, `all`, `once` source section, absence/uniqueness/collection, joint
condition, dynamic space, host-space form the occurrence view cannot retain,
or another effectful generation segment keeps the existing eager/native route.
These are evaluation barriers, not blanket unsupported-feature errors. Public
materialization and full-drain behavior remain available for the complete
language surface. No effectful segment is moved across a firing to obtain
laziness.

A returned proposal owns its syntax independently of scratch bindings. The
cursor releases every level, occurrence pin, retained environment and arena
exactly once on exhaustion, error, interruption or early close. Publication of
an answer precedes closing storage it borrows. Cancellation is not rollback.
Native fallback is permitted only before the first firing; after an answerless
firing, a later producer, clone, freshening or allocation decline is terminal
and handled, so it cannot restart and repeat the published prefix.

Independent calibration and negative controls
---------------------------------------------

The Lean `Demand` and `SearchCursor` contracts supply the semantic controls:
first answer after an answerless effectful prefix, `collapse(once(P))`,
answer-order sensitivity, HE all-error multiplicity and later-success
preference, PeTTa raise sequencing, and publish-then-close ownership. They do
not establish C correspondence.

The independent HE library reference, Rust HE observations, PeTTa differential,
profile tests, native fuel controls and owned resumable-consumer gates remain
the executable references. Raw `eval`/`evalc` retaining an `Empty` invocation
value while `metta` exposes an empty answer frontier is a required negative
control for any shared frontier adapter.

Measurements distinguish candidate production, occurrence-row scans, proposal
allocations, cursor/view retention, first-answer work and full-drain throughput.
Entry data/index retention is not answer materialization, and constant retained
answer storage is not a claim of constant-time search.
